## Types of cover worth discussing with a broker

- **Cyber incident response costs** — the direct cost of responding to a
  breach (forensic investigation, legal counsel, PR).
- **Privacy/data breach liability** — third-party claims arising from a
  data breach, particularly relevant given the wellbeing-sensitive
  content this product processes (`DATA_INVENTORY.md`).
- **Regulatory defence and fines** — legal costs defending an ICO
  investigation, and (where insurable — fines themselves often aren't)
  associated costs.
- **Forensic investigation costs** — specialist costs to determine what
  happened after an incident.
- **Notification costs** — the cost of notifying affected individuals
  and regulators, which for a wellbeing product with many active users
  could be non-trivial if ever triggered.
- **Business interruption** — lost revenue from a cyber incident taking
  the service down.
- **Ransomware / cyber extortion** — relevant even for a Google Cloud-
  hosted service, since credential compromise (not just infrastructure
  compromise) is a live attack vector.
- **Third-party liability** — if a vendor integration or a security
  failure on Blaze Break's side causes loss to a customer organisation.
- **Social engineering** — fraud induced via impersonation/phishing
  rather than a technical exploit.

## What this document does not do

- It does not recommend a specific insurer or broker.
- It does not estimate a premium or coverage amount — that depends on
  factors (user count, data sensitivity, revenue, broker risk
  assessment) outside this document's scope.
- It does not claim coverage exists. If/when a policy is obtained, this
  document should be updated with the real policy summary (insurer,
  coverage limits, renewal date) so it becomes an accurate record rather
  than a checklist.
